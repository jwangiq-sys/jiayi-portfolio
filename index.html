The journey

From frameworks
to professional judgement.

The internship showed me that strong risk work is not just about producing the right number. It is about understanding what drives it, testing whether it makes sense, and making the result useful to others.

  1. 01

    Understand

    Learned the business context, project objectives, data definitions, and the logic behind each analytical framework.

  2. 02

    Build

    Turned requirements into structured workbooks, calculation flows, checks, and concise working documentation.

  3. 03

    Challenge

    Compared outputs, investigated unexpected movements, and learned when to question an assumption or escalate an issue.

  4. 04

    Communicate

    Presented analysis in a way that connected technical detail to the decision or risk question at hand.

Selected work

Two projects.
One sharper lens.

Examples are intentionally anonymized and described at a high level to protect client confidentiality.

01

Risk resilience · Bank

Learning stress testing
for a bank

The question

How can a bank evaluate its resilience when economic and market conditions move away from the expected path?

What I practised

  • Tracing scenario assumptions through a structured analytical process
  • Reviewing data consistency and investigating unusual movements
  • Connecting model outputs to an understandable risk narrative
Key takeaway

A stress test is most useful when the numbers, assumptions, and business interpretation tell one coherent story.

02

Regulatory capital · Securitization

Independently calculating
RWA for securitization

The question

How can a complex regulatory capital calculation be rebuilt into a transparent, reviewable sequence?

What I practised

  • Breaking a technical calculation into clear inputs, logic, and outputs
  • Performing independent checks and reconciling results
  • Documenting assumptions so another reviewer could follow the work
Key takeaway

Independence is not working in isolation; it is owning the logic, validating the result, and knowing when to ask a precise question.

Professional growth

What changed
in how I work.

A

Structured thinking

I learned to begin with the decision and work backwards: define the question, map the logic, identify inputs, and design checks before polishing the output.

B

Technical ownership

I became more comfortable working through unfamiliar methods, finding gaps in my understanding, and building an evidence trail for each conclusion.

C

Professional communication

I learned to tailor the level of detail: concise for progress updates, explicit for reviewers, and intuitive when explaining technical ideas to a wider audience.

D

Quality under pressure

I developed habits that protect quality under deadlines—version control, visible checks, early escalation, and a final review from the reader’s perspective.

Reflection

“

The best analysis makes complexity easier to trust.

Before this internship, I often thought of technical competence as the ability to reach an answer. I now see it as a wider responsibility: understand the purpose of the work, make the reasoning traceable, test the result, and communicate what matters.

The two projects developed different parts of that responsibility. Stress testing trained me to think across scenarios and interpret change. The securitization RWA work trained me to slow down, unpack a complex process, and take ownership of every step. Together, they made me more disciplined, more curious, and more confident in asking better questions.

My next step is to deepen my understanding of financial risk frameworks while continuing to improve the tools that turn analysis into action: concise writing, clear visualization, and professional judgement.
